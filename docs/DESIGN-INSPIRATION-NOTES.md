## Shared Patterns (What All Four Do Well)

### 1. Lead capture is the #1 job

- Hero headline + subheadline + primary CTA ("Free Estimate", "Get A Free Quote")
- Lead form appears early — often in or directly below the hero
- Phone number visible in header and repeated in sticky mobile bar
- Multiple CTA touchpoints throughout the page (not just one at the bottom)
- Footer form on some sites (Amarillo)

### 2. Trust-first messaging

- Anti-gimmick positioning: "Without The Games & Gimmicks", "Pressure-Free", "Honest Answers"
- Specific credibility claims: years in business, jobs completed, certifications
- "Why Choose Us" sections with 4–6 icon/card blocks
- Real named reviews with partial quotes (not generic lorem ipsum)
- Team profiles with experience stats (Arlington AC does this exceptionally)

### 3. Deep service architecture

- Mega-menu or dropdown nav with service categories and sub-services
- Each major service gets its own page + often a gallery page
- Service cards on homepage link out to dedicated landing pages
- Internal linking between services, areas, blog, and projects

### 4. SEO content engine

- Blog with tabbed views: Recent / Popular / Case Studies
- City-specific blog posts (e.g., "Siding in Hereford, TX", "AC Service in Mansfield, TX")
- Case studies that tell customer stories, not just product specs
- Service area pages and footer service area lists
- Quick Summary blocks at top of blog posts

### 5. Visual structure (homepage flow)

Typical section order across sites:

1. Hero + form
2. Trust / promise statement
3. Why Choose Us (cards)
4. Services overview (cards with images)
5. About / company story snippet
6. Team (Arlington) or Process (OC Boston) or Credentials (Willi)
7. Reviews carousel
8. Blog preview (3-tab)
9. Service areas
10. Final CTA / form
11. Footer (contact, menu, social, legal)

### 6. Footer & utility

- NAP (Name, Address, Phone) consistent
- Terms, Privacy, Sitemap links
- Social links
- "Website By Level 10 Contractor" credit
- Mobile: sticky call / text / chat bar

### 7. Conversion extras (use selectively)

- Financing page/link (Amarillo, Arlington, OC Boston, Willi)
- Exit-intent or timed popup forms (Arlington AC, Willi)
- Appointment booking (OC Boston has calendar integration)
- Awards & associations pages

## Site-by-Site Notes

### Amarillo Solar Shades — Closest structural match for Elite Builders

**Why it matters most:** Exterior remodeling contractor with siding overlap, service areas, and gallery-heavy content — very similar business model to Elite Builders (minus framing as primary).

**Standout elements:**

- Hero: "Stunning Exterior Remodels… Without The Games & Gimmicks"
- Product partnership callout (Infinity by Marvin) — equivalent for Elite: licensed/insured, quality materials, trade certifications
- Four service pillars on homepage: Windows, Doors, Siding, Outdoor Living → maps to Framing, Siding, Decks, General Contracting
- Each service card has a sub-headline, description, and two links (Services + Gallery)
- "Why Homeowners Choose" — 6 expandable/trust cards
- Five-star reviews section with "Check Out More Reviews" link
- Blog tabs: Recent / Popular / Case Studies
- Service area footer blurb listing cities

**Take for Elite Builders:**

- Service card layout (image + headline + short copy + dual CTAs)
- Gallery per service (framing gallery, deck gallery, etc.)
- Regional blog content strategy (already in SEO plan)
- "No games" trust tone fits contractor frustration with bad builders

### Arlington AC & Heating — Best for trust, team, and anti-pressure tone

**Why it matters:** Strongest "why trust us" narrative and team humanization.

**Standout elements:**

- Hero bullet points: Fast Response, Honest Answers, Guaranteed Workmanship
- Inline testimonial quote right under hero ("I can't scream this loud enough…")
- **Meet Our Team** section with modals: photo, role, years experience, jobs done, customer quote
- Owner story with personal mission (Ted Jessup) — builds deep trust
- "Why DFW Homeowners Trust" — 5 linked trust pillars with dedicated sub-pages
- Core values as standalone pages (Integrity, trust, reliability, etc.)
- Stats: "14,000+ projects/year", "since 1973", "25+ years experience"
- Warranty specifics called out (10-year installs, 1-year repairs)

**Take for Elite Builders:**

- Team section if Elite has identifiable leads/foremen (huge differentiator for framing)
- Hero trust bullets (Licensed & Insured, On-Time Scheduling, Quality Craftsmanship)
- Owner/founder story on About page
- Specific warranty or guarantee language if applicable
- "Even our new guys have 25 years" style experience framing

**Caution:**

- Some review text on the site appears mismatched (window install reviews on HVAC site) — don't replicate content quality issues

### Owens Corning Boston (Lux Renovations) — Best for process and authority positioning

**Why it matters:** Shows how to sell a specialized, high-trust construction service with a premium partner brand.

**Standout elements:**

- Exclusive provider positioning ("Exclusive Provider Of The Owens Corning Basement Finishing System")
- Multi-state service area in hero subheadline
- **Process section** — 4 clear steps with expandable detail:
  1. Design & Discovery Meeting
  2. Project Management & Permits
  3. Installation (timeline: as little as 3 weeks)
  4. Final Walk-Throughs
- "10,000+ installs" social proof
- Gallery with Before & After emphasis
- Pressure-free sales explicitly called out multiple times
- Financing available callout
- Blog organized by room/use-case tags (Home Gyms, Game Rooms, Living Areas)

**Take for Elite Builders:**

- **Process section** is highly relevant for framing (design → permit → frame → inspection → walkthrough)
- Project count / years in business stats
- Before/after project gallery (aligns with SEO plan Phase 6)
- "What to expect" content reduces fear for homeowners hiring a framer
- Exclusive/specialized positioning — Elite could own "framing specialist" vs general handyman

### Willi Roofing — Best for local authority and product depth

**Why it matters:** Strong local SEO positioning, certification badges, and educational (not salesy) consultation approach.

**Standout elements:**

- Credential in headline: "GAF Master Elite… Top 2% Of Roofers Nationally"
- Hero form with address field (not just zip) — higher intent for roofing; consider for framing estimates
- **Complete Systems** positioning — not just shingles, full stack (underlayment, decking inspection, ventilation)
- Equivalent for Elite: "complete framing package" vs piecemeal labor
- Five trust cards: No-Haggle Prices, No Sales Pressure, Educational Meetings, Top Materials, Master Elite Certified
- About snippet with "25+ years" and clear value props
- Service area list with "…And More!" linking to full areas page
- Financing section
- Blog heavy on case studies and local city posts (Dripping Springs, Kyle, Round Rock)

**Take for Elite Builders:**

- Lead with a single sharp credential line in hero (Licensed CA Contractor, X years, X projects)
- "We don't cover up problems — we fix them" → translates perfectly to framing (structural honesty)
- Educational consultation positioning vs hard sell
- City-named blog posts (already in SEO plan)
- Fair/transparent pricing messaging

## Design / UX Observations

### What works

- Scannable sections with clear H2 headings
- Short paragraphs, lots of white space between sections
- Service cards use images — critical for construction sites
- Repeated CTAs without feeling spammy (because sections alternate content vs conversion)
- Local geo mentions woven into headlines naturally

### What to watch out for

- **Loading screens** — all four show "Loading" initially; avoid this on Elite (bad for LCP and UX)
- **Template sameness** — same footer structure, same blog tab pattern, same form fields
- **Long pages** — homepage is very long; use anchor nav or clear section rhythm so mobile doesn't feel endless
- **Popup fatigue** — Arlington and Willi use exit popups; can annoy users and hurt INP scores
- **Form length** — keep initial form short (name, phone, zip, message); ask for more on follow-up call
- **Stock-feel copy** — some sections read formulaic; Elite copy should reference real Modesto/Central Valley specifics

## Summary Opinion

**These are excellent inspiration choices.** They represent a battle-tested contractor website formula that clearly converts and ranks. Amarillo Solar Shades is the closest cousin to Elite Builders structurally. Arlington AC has the best trust/team storytelling. OC Boston nails process transparency. Willi Roofing nails local authority and educational selling.

The biggest opportunity for Elite Builders isn't copying the look — it's copying the **structure and SEO depth** while building something **faster, framing-focused, and CMS-driven** through the admin already in progress. That combination could genuinely outperform typical Central Valley contractor sites and even these inspiration sites on Core Web Vitals.

**Do not clone blindly.** Extract the playbook, then give Elite Builders its own visual identity and framing-first positioning.
